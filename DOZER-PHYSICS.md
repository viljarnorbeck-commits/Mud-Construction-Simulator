# Bulldozer contact, draft and blade mechanism

## Root causes and changes

Machines use the custom terrain/contact drivetrain, not Chaos vehicle wheels. Their environment box blocks dynamic physics bodies such as fallen trees and ignores static terrain. Thus the small-mound stops did not come from a hidden Chaos blade box or duplicate soil rigid bodies: the underside test fitted a plane through the track endpoints and rejected a crest between them as an immovable obstacle. The old pose also sampled the ground at the chassis centre rather than both complete track footprints.

`TrackGround::Fit` now supports tracked machines on both full contact strips, including rounded idler approaches. Crests lift the supported frame; a hard ridge in the central gap can still ground the actual belly. The same support model drives tracked poses and underside clearance. Fifteen weighted soil/grip samples per track replace the single centre sample. Actual shoe width determines pressure, metre-grid contacts integrate subcell coverage, and lower shoes have bounded visual compliance in depressions. This shared correction also applies to the excavator. Wheeled poses now use the actual support rows instead of treating a central heap as an extra wheel; their underside probe uses tyre support width and the narrower chassis envelope.

A further shared mismatch was that the reconstructed surface touched a tool or chassis before a metre-spaced cell centre entered its footprint. Swept tools, grading and body pressure now integrate 4×4 subcell coverage, including intersecting edge cells. Soft material under the belly can compact under its share of supported weight. Force is distributed over actual contact cells and removed from driven-contact normal load; packing remains bounded and preserves mass. A narrow loose crest can settle, while hard aggregate remains an obstruction. There is no arbitrary soft-material collision bypass.

The former bulldozer speed subtraction is replaced by external draft resistance in the existing drivetrain force balance. Resistance includes material-dependent passive earth pressure, cohesion, depth, blade width/angle, moisture and sliding wedge mass. Units are kN and tonnes, so draft divided by supported machine mass gives acceleration. Engine-force constants are unchanged. The soil wedge is supported by the ground; it is no longer mistakenly treated as carried payload that increases the machine's weight and reduces its engine acceleration. Other vehicles still carry their actual cargo. The loader had the same flow-rate-to-speed penalty; its cutting edge now uses the shared `SoilDraft` law rather than a smoothed activity signal or an unconditional full-bucket stop. Excess load can stall the drivetrain on firm ground or cause bandspin when grip is insufficient. Reversing releases the forward cutting draft. The dozer HUD reports heavy draft and suggests a shallower cut.

The previous two long push arms were tied to unrotated blade-side pins, while the blade rotated about a different point. Rear braces also extended through the moldboard profile. The model now has a rigid C-frame, a common rear articulation pin and a separate yaw carrier. Outside push arms clear the tracks; a fork converges on the central joint ahead of the idlers. Lift cylinders connect to that fork, angling cylinders connect to blade ears, and the tilt cylinder follows the yaw carrier. Every endpoint follows its parent transform and every cylinder retains its physical attachment. Rear braces remain behind the curved panel. Decorative scratch triangles that looked like protruding metal have been removed.

Lift is limited to -0.28..1.05 m, yaw to ±25°, and roll tilt to ±8°. Limits also apply when loading older positions. Hold either mouse button and drag to lift/angle; hold both buttons and drag sideways to tilt. Existing vertical mouse preference remains controlled by the operator input mapping. Blade tilt reuses the saved tilt channel, so no world reset or save migration is needed.

Clearance, cutting pressure and swept excavation use the transformed cutting edge, including the curved wings and lowered side. Twelve swept strips remove real column material and feed the conserved wedge/spill loads. Grading combines the strips into one weighted working area so material can transfer across the full blade rather than treating each narrow strip as an isolated grader. The shared grader respects the physical cutting plane when removing compacted layers. Side flow and spreading follow blade angle and tilt, and the ground-supported visible bank follows blade yaw. Raising the blade initiates finite, conserved spreading of the unsupported wedge; after emptying, the blade stays at the operator's chosen height.

V now holds the surveyed reference height until disengaged or overridden by manual controls. Repeatedly sampling the dozer's own freshly lowered strip caused a feedback loop that deepened the trench on each pass. The HUD labels this mode “Grade hold”.

Terrain rendering and collision continue to share the bounded reconstructed surface. No collision channel was disabled and no replacement decorative cliff/pile colliders were introduced. The drivetrain queries the authoritative deformed terrain directly; the native tests separately check the updated Chaos terrain surface.

## Verification

`bash tools/run-physics-tests.sh` runs ten C++ suites. The new suite checks 1,089 lift/yaw/tilt poses, a fixed articulation centre, C-frame/moldboard separation, cylinder envelope and panel clearance, distributed track support, retained centre-rock obstruction, draft progression, motor stall and low-grip bandspin. Existing loader, running gear, worker, terrain reconstruction, transport, mass and generated-road tests also pass.

`tools/start-highlands.ps1 -Mode Validate` includes native A–H bulldozer acceptance. An earlier candidate with the final C-frame dimensions and shared support/draft implementation passed: 30.05 m over four track mounds and one narrow centre mound without rolling or a false stop; 12.34 m of progressive pushing, including a 1.01 m cut at the pile centre; zero mass error; approximately zero progress under a deliberately excessive soil load; side deposition centroids -2.14/+2.15 m. Articulation pins, cutting-edge coordinates, rod endpoints and cylinder lengths were correct (42.2..188.5 cm across all cylinders), and updated terrain collision/restoration passed. Work-volume diagnostics now record actual per-step transport rather than integrating the smoothed HUD activity signal; transported material can be handled more than once.

That candidate also passed the native game regression: manual dozer excavation (2.919 m3 held), 4 cm windrows on both wings with a full blade, grading (5 cm reduction), and other machines' digging, loading, tipping, steering and controls. The loader-to-dumper and loader-to-truck acceptance cycles passed, with 1.227 m3 transferred and zero mass error. The manual fixture resets prior mouse-test blade angles and actually lowers the cutting edge; the full-blade fixture feeds a shallow windrow instead of requiring a full machine to ram an abrupt tall gravel wall. The overload case remains separate.

The extended soil-lab test exposed a separate failure: its loader approached below the cut face after automatic grading followed its own newly excavated surface. The final source holds a fixed surveyed grade, adds a regression against self-deepening, and approaches the soil face using the normal hydraulic controls and measured tooth depth. These last corrections are compiled but their native acceptance is **pending**, not passed. The previous extended soil-lab run failed the straight/right-angle scoop cases; its dozer push, blade-raise deposit, save and conservation checks passed.

Final verification on 2026-11-20: all ten portable C++ suites passed, and the final C++ module compiled and linked with Visual Studio. Unreal's rules assembly was blocked by Windows App Control, so the existing generated MSVC compilation/link response files were used without executing that rules assembly. Windows then rejected loading the final game DLL itself with `GetLastError=4551`. A later retry of the same unchanged DLL was also blocked before gameplay. Final native world, regression, soil-lab and loader checks therefore remain pending. No security policy was disabled or relaxed, and no renamed DLL was used to evade the block.

`tools/render-material-review.ps1 -Dozer -Offscreen` captures both maximum blade angles/tilts and the lowered blade; it rejects failed or stale captures. Earlier geometry views were inspected and led to the wider frame/fork correction. The final wider frame is covered by the 1,089-pose geometry suite, but its final rendered review remains pending the Windows load block. A preliminary earlier-candidate 1080p work scene measured 162.0 fps idle and 56.9 fps during work; it predates the last shared contact changes and is **not** a final performance result. Local diagnostics are in `Saved/EarthworksTests`; older render/performance folders must not be treated as final-build evidence.

The model remains a heightfield with custom tracked support and approximate earth-pressure coefficients, not a complete rigid-body suspension, calibrated soil mechanics solver or particle DEM simulation. Very large piles still need shallower cuts and repeated passes. Heavy-work performance and 4K/120 fps remain ongoing limitations. No GPU soil simulation or CPU/GPU readback dependency was added.
