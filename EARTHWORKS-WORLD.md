# Earthworks world

The default world is now `world/earthworks-42`, ID `earthworks-v3-42`. It has its own local save profile; existing Highlands and Valley saves are retained and are never applied to the new elevation field. Use `tools/start-highlands.ps1 -World highlands-42` to revisit the previous world, or `-World earthworks-42` for the new one. Windows builds and installers select Earthworks by default.

Regenerate with `node tools/generate-highlands.mjs 42 world/earthworks-42 --earthworks`. The old generator and world remain reproducible without that flag.

The 2 km square terrain combines warped multifractal ridges, sediment terraces with sharp slope breaks, incised tributary gullies and secondary erosion fingers. These are procedural erosion-inspired landforms, not a hydraulic erosion simulation. There are floodplains, steep folded uplands, loose supplies, saturated clay hollows and streams. The flat machinery yard is at (155,1640); the first delivery site is across the earth bank and ravine at (565,1630). The bank begins roughly 80 m east of the yard. There are several possible crossing locations along it and further independent earthworks challenges across the map. No routes are inserted as road meshes or unlocked by mission flags.

The delivery site sits on an eroded sediment terrace remnant, with an eleven-metre excavatable margin. This closes the easy flat detour around the initial bank and ravine. A world-wide, eight-neighbour route search with a generous 35% uphill limit verifies that the unworked delivery site is unreachable; players choose where to cut a ramp or build a fill embankment around its perimeter. This search is an offline acceptance check, not a runtime access restriction.

Thick till and clay remain excavatable on steep faces. Geology explicitly defines material and depth; distant rock outcrops have the existing greater excavation resistance. The old slope-based rock fallback remains for older worlds, but explicit geology overrides it throughout Earthworks. Soil, gravel and rock masses, packing, rutting, moisture and save serialization continue to use the existing column model. Deep columns now retain a valid rock foundation even when soil depth exceeds 32 m.

Vehicle motion retains traction, load, sinkage, side-slope rollover and water limits. A width-aware underside probe now checks the actual terrain between support rows, accounting for chassis clearance, load, sinkage and articulated rear-frame position. Convex crests can ground a long truck while a short tracked machine can work closer to them. Reversing out of grounding is permitted. This is a kinematic support model, not a full suspension/contact rigid-body solver.

The physical grid remains 1 m, with the existing bounded half-metre surface reconstruction shared by rendering and collision near machinery. Sharp earth slopes use deformable terrain triangles, not decorative uneditable cliff meshes. Overhangs and tunnels are outside this heightfield representation.

## Verification

Run `node tests/earthworks-world.mjs`, `node tests/highlands-world.mjs` and `bash tools/run-physics-tests.sh` (WSL on Windows). The generated CSV fixture is cross-checked against the real 16-bit heightmap. The C++ acceptance drives a loaded truck across that terrain using the production drivetrain and clearance probe, then cuts and fills its columns, adds a finite gravel stock and compacts with the production material model. It checks improvement and mass conservation. This deterministic acceptance does not replace an Unreal gameplay run with the actual implements, foliage and collision.

`tools/start-highlands.ps1 -Mode Validate` runs native world/vehicle tests; `-Mode Regression` runs the game smoke test. Windows App Control has previously blocked unsigned Unreal game modules with error 4551 on this computer. Successful compilation or deterministic physics tests must not be reported as proof that this policy permits starting the new game. See `TERRAIN-RENDERING.md` for the existing runtime restriction.

### Results, 20 November 2026

- New and legacy world tests passed. Earthworks has elevations 6.67–309.52 m, 32.08% of samples with gradient above 0.7 (legacy 3.25%), 39.34% relatively flat ground, 21,202 trees and 3,825 boulders. There are verified steep earth banks and ravines at five independent near-yard crossing locations. The whole-world route search confirms that the delivery terrace needs earthwork.
- All nine C++ suites passed. On the generated physical profile, the 80%-loaded FMX made no forward progress before work (rolled back 6.73 m); after cut/fill, finite gravel reinforcement and compaction it travelled 466.02 m, passing the delivery terrace. Approximately 41,992 m3 was excavated across this complete 11 m wide test corridor; mass error was about 2e-8 tonnes. This broad acceptance corridor is not a prescribed or optimized player route. Tests also exercise width, tracked-versus-wheeled clearance and reversing off a grounding.
- Unreal Engine 5.7 compilation, final native world validation and game regression passed. Chaos tests verified tree fall, undercutting, real collision and disk restoration. Production truck travel was 14.46 m on graded ground, zero on wet clay and 18.05 m after reinforcement; a tracked dozer travelled 18 m on clay. On the generated 499% earth bank, production excavation removed 6 m3 of soft material, deposited it in the real ravine and round-tripped the changed columns through saving/restoration with zero mass error.
- Windows temporarily rejected the final unsigned module with error 4551. Subsequent regression and world validation passed using the identical, unchanged module and normal security settings. This does not guarantee that Windows will trust an unsigned distribution on another computer. No new 4K/120 fps or packaged installer claim is made.
- Native GPU material review rendered all three 1920×1080 views successfully. Its measured mean was 114.2 fps before work and 20.3 fps during the combined five-material heap/dozer/continuous-pouring stress sequence (p95 frame times 13.50/62.37 ms). Heavy active earthwork remains a performance limitation; this is not a 120 fps result. These fixture views inspect material surfaces and deformation near the yard, not the full terrain panorama. Artifacts and logs are in `Saved/EarthworksTests/Render` on the development machine.
